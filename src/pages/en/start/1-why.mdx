## Maybe this has happened to you

You ask an AI to make a small web page, or a small browser plug-in. It answers fast: "Done!"

You open it, and the button does nothing. You go back and say "still broken." It says "sorry, fixed now." You open it again. Same thing. After a few rounds you start wondering whether you explained it badly.

It isn't your fault. **When the AI says "done," it never opened the thing to look.**

## pstack makes it work like a reliable repair person

Think of calling someone to fix a leaking tap:

- An unreliable one gives it a couple of turns, says "fixed," and leaves.
- A reliable one turns the tap on first to see exactly where it leaks. After the repair, they turn it on again so you can see with your own eyes that it doesn't leak. Only then are they done.

pstack is a "reliable repair person's code" you install into the AI. With it, the AI picks up three habits:

1. **It repeats your request back.** It says in its own words what you want. If it got it wrong, you can correct it right away.
2. **It finds where the problem is before changing anything.**
3. **It shows proof when it's done.** A screenshot, say, or what happened when it tried the thing itself.

The third one helps most if you don't code: <mark><strong>you don't have to understand the code, only the proof.</strong></mark>

## What you need

- **Claude Code, and you can already chat with the AI in it.** If you haven't installed it, follow [Claude Code's official quickstart](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/quickstart) first, then come back.
- **A paid Claude plan.** Claude Code needs one anyway.

That's all. You don't need to know how to program, or learn any other tool first.

## It's a bit slower and uses more of your plan

That's because it checks and confirms more. So skip it for small things:

- **Fixing a typo, changing a color**: just ask the AI.
- **Making something new, fixing a problem that keeps coming back**: use pstack.
